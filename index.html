Mission briefing

The host reads this out at the start. It is the whole story in sixty seconds.

Nineteenth of August, 1960, five in the morning. Satellite 9009 has just ejected its film bucket. It was supposed to come down in the Pacific. It came down here. The catch aircraft missed, the bucket broke open on impact, and five film cassettes are scattered across this campus, each with a radio beacon and a battery that lasts thirty minutes. Inside is the first film ever returned from orbit. Someone else is listening for those beacons too.

You are five recovery crews. Your recovery order came in by teleprinter, authenticated with your crew codeword, and the photo-interpreters write codewords the only way they ever write anything: in landforms shaped like letters. Three direction-finding stations on campus heard your cassette's beacon. Their report is enciphered with your codeword.

Decode the codeword. Decrypt the report. Plot the three bearings. Where the lines cross, your cassette is down. Report the building to Recovery Control and you get your strip of the film. Five strips make the photograph. Thirty minutes. Go.

The real event behind the story

On 19 August 1960 the CORONA reconnaissance satellite Discoverer 14 (mission 9009) ejected its film-return capsule on orbit 17. A C-119 aircraft, call sign Pelican 9, caught it in mid-air on the third pass, about 580 km south-west of Hawaii. It was the first time photographs had ever been recovered from orbit. The capsule was located by parachute and radio beacon.

In the game the capsule lands on the University of Twente campus instead, and the recovery is run from Langezijds, the ITC building, which gives the operation its codename.

What you get

You play in crews of three, all at the table in room 2405. Recovery Control is the host's desk. Each crew gets one envelope and shares the table tools.

In your envelope

  • 11–12 Landsat frames, each a landform shaped like a letter, unnumbered
  • Your crew card (A4, double-sided): the enciphered report, your three bearings, the rules
  • A Vigenère table

On the table

  • An A3 campus map with building numbers (page-up is north)
  • Protractor, ruler, pencil

Four steps in a chain, then a finale with all crews. Each step's answer is what you need for the next one, so do them in order.

[1] CODEWORDLandform letters spell your crew codeword
[2] DECRYPTThe codeword unlocks the direction-finding report
[3] PLOTThree bearings on the campus map cross at one building

What to do, step by step

The same instructions as on your crew card. Your own cipher and bearings are only on the card.

Step 1 · Find your codeword about 5 min

Use: the Landsat frames. Get: your KEY for step 2.

  1. Each frame shows one letter, upper or lower case. You may need to rotate a frame.
  2. Arrange the letters into a three-word phrase about looking at the Earth. The word lengths are on your card (for example 4 / 2 / 5).
  3. Write the phrase WITHOUT spaces. That is your KEY.

Step 2 · Decrypt the report about 5 min

Use: the cipher on your card, your KEY, the Vigenère table. Get: three two-digit building numbers, the DF stations ALPHA, BRAVO and CHARLIE.

  1. Write your KEY under the cipher, letter by letter, repeating the key when it runs out. Split the text between the three of you.
  2. For each letter: find the KEY letter's row in the table, find the CIPHER letter in that row, read the PLAIN letter at the top of that column. (Example: cipher D with key E gives Z.)
  3. The plaintext spells SIX digits as English words (ZERO, ONE, TWO …). Read them as three two-digit building numbers, in order: ALPHA, BRAVO, CHARLIE.

Cipher on the card looks like XXXXX XXXXX XXXXX XXXXX XX. One wrong letter still reads as a number word, so do not restart if something looks slightly off.

Step 3 · Plot the bearings about 5 min

Use: the A3 map, protractor, ruler, the three bearings on your card. Get: one building number, where your cassette is.

  1. Find station ALPHA's black number circle on the map.
  2. Put the protractor's centre on that circle with 0° pointing up the page (north). Mark the bearing, measured clockwise from north, and draw a long line through the circle and the mark.
  3. Repeat for BRAVO and CHARLIE. Two lines find the building, the third confirms it.
  4. The three lines cross on ONE black building number. That is your answer.

Bearings on the card look like ALPHA → ___° BRAVO → ___° CHARLIE → ___°.

Step 4 · Report to Recovery Control

  1. Walk to Recovery Control and say your crew number and the building number. One building number per attempt.
  2. Correct: your finish time is logged and you receive your film strip. Wrong: +2 minutes on your score, back to the map.
  3. Ground Truth bonus. The host picks up to three of your Landsat frames and asks where on Earth each was taken. Each correct country or region takes 1 minute off your score. This is also the tiebreaker.

Step 5 · Splice the film all crews, 3 min

At 24:00 everyone comes to the splicing table with their strip. Each strip is a vertical slice of one Landsat scene with about 2 cm of overlap with its neighbours. Align the strips along the overlaps, like a mosaic, and the letters printed on them spell a 13-letter message. The host reads the closing line and announces the ranking.

Rules & scoring

Your score = clock time of your correct submission + 2 min per wrong submission + 1 min per paid hint − 1 min per Ground Truth answer (max 3). Lowest score wins.

  • Not finished by 24:00: you score 24:00 plus penalties and are ranked after all finished crews, by steps completed.
  • Ties: more Ground Truth answers wins; then the earlier step 2 completion.
  • Free hints are read out to the whole room at 9:00, 14:00 and 19:00. You can also ask Recovery Control for a hint at any time; that costs 1 minute.
  • Keep your cipher and bearings to yourselves. Every crew has a different target, so there is nothing to copy anyway.
