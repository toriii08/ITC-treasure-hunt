The real event behind the story

On 19 August 1960 the CORONA reconnaissance satellite Discoverer 14 (mission 9009) ejected its film-return capsule on orbit 17. A C-119 aircraft, call sign Pelican 9, caught it in mid-air on the third pass, about 580 km south-west of Hawaii. It was the first time photographs had ever been recovered from orbit. The capsule was located by parachute and radio beacon.

In the game the capsule lands on the University of Twente campus instead, and the recovery is run from Langezijds, the ITC building, which gives the operation its codename.

Step 1, codeword

The frames are deliberately unnumbered, so the crew must both recognise the letters and order them. Word lengths and the theme keep it a warm-up, not a wall. Target 4–5 minutes.

Step 2, decrypt

An 11–12 letter key cannot be brute-forced, but three people can split the text and work in parallel. Spelled-out numbers are error-tolerant: one wrong letter still reads as THREE or NINE. Target 4–6 minutes.

Step 3, plot the bearings

This is the "aha" of the game. Crossing angles are 54°–59° or better (good GDOP), and every target is at least 3 cm from any other numbered circle on the A3 print, so a ±2° protractor error never lands on a neighbour. The five targets are all different, so crews cannot copy. Target 4–6 minutes.

Bearings were computed from the map image, so the host must verify them once with a protractor on the real A3 print before the event. The host area draws the lines for each crew.

Step 4 and the Ground Truth bonus

The bonus is the tiebreaker and keeps fast crews busy while slower crews finish.

Step 5, splice

A cooperative finale absorbs the time gap between fast and slow crews. Simplified version: no overlaps; each strip carries its building number on the back and the strips are ordered by number. Expected finish: fastest crew 15–17 min, slowest 22–24 min.

Design principles

  • Each step uses a different mechanic and takes about five minutes.
  • Information gating, not physical gating: everything happens at the table.
  • Every element handed out is used; nothing is decoration.
  • Answers are unique. A wrong guess costs time, so guessing is slower than solving.
  • A cooperative meta-finale absorbs the time gap between fast and slow crews.
  • All rules are printed on the crew card. Nothing is explained ad hoc.
